Ideology is more complicated than left vs. right.

This assessment separates your views about economics, culture, civil liberty, federalism, foreign policy, institutions, and democratic reform. It then compares those beliefs—separately—to contemporary American party coalitions.

Important: This is an educational tool, not a scientific personality test, voter recommendation, or party-registration tool. It does not send or store your answers. All 30 questions and their answer choices are randomized each time to reduce response-pattern effects.
You do not need to already know AP Gov vocabulary. Read each choice for the basic idea behind it and choose the one that comes closest to what you believe. If none is perfect, pick the best fit.

Your results include: ideological family; center-left/right direction; ideological intensity; cross-pressure/consistency; seven dimensions; independent party compatibility; American thinkers; and a contemporary political-neighborhood section.

Your Political Profile

Direction, Intensity & Consistency

Direction asks which way your policy answers tend to lean. Intensity measures how far from the middle your answers tend to be. Consistency distinguishes a genuinely centrist pattern from a cross-pressured mix of stronger views from different traditions.

Your Seven Dimensions

Closest Ideological Traditions

These percentages are similarity estimates based on the distance between your seven-dimensional profile and each broad ideological tradition. They are not party-membership scores and do not mean you agree with every idea in that tradition.

American Thinkers & Political Figures

These are idea comparisons, not endorsements and not claims that you agree with everything a person believed.

Party Compatibility

These are independent compatibility scores. The leading party is no longer automatically shown as 100%. Each percentage asks how closely your answers match that party's coded positions across the questions in this quiz.

Your Closest Contemporary Political Matches

These politicians are matched individually to your seven-dimensional ideological profile, so they can come from different parties. The comparison pool includes a wider set of current national figures plus Georgia officials, including the current representative for Georgia's 10th District. A percentage indicates similarity on the dimensions measured here—not endorsement, personality similarity, or agreement on every issue.

Where You Don't Fit

AP Gov Reflection

Use the results shown above. You do not need to remember individual quiz questions. The goal is to explain what your overall profile suggests about political ideology and your own developing views.

1. Look at your overall ideological profile and seven dimensions. What stands out to you most? Was the result mostly what you expected, or did anything surprise you? Explain.

2. Look at your strongest two or three dimensions. What values seem to matter most in shaping your political views—for example liberty, equality, order, tradition, opportunity, security, local control, or effective government? Explain using your results.

3. Compare your ideological profile with your party compatibility scores. How closely do they line up? What does your result suggest about the difference between a person's ideology and a political party?

4. Read your “Where You Don't Fit” section. What does it show about the usefulness—and the limits—of labels such as liberal, conservative, moderate, Democrat, or Republican?

5. Political socialization is the process through which people develop political beliefs. What influences do you think have shaped your views so far? How open do you think your views are to changing as you learn more in AP Government?

6. Look at your “Closest Contemporary Political Matches.” Which politician seems most interesting or surprising as a comparison? Use your ideological dimensions to explain why that match may make sense, and identify at least one reason the comparison is still imperfect.

How is this scored?

Ideology and party compatibility are calculated independently. Ideological dimensions use values from −2 to +2. Ideological families are determined by distance between your seven-dimensional profile and several broad ideological prototypes. Party compatibility uses a separate 0–4 fit code for every response and every party; your score is the percentage of the maximum compatibility available for the questions you answered. No ideological label adds or subtracts party points. Politician matches are calculated separately by comparing your seven-dimensional profile with each politician's broad ideological profile, so matches can cross party lines. Question and answer order are randomized when the quiz begins.

Party coding is a classroom simplification based primarily on current official party platforms and issue statements. Parties are coalitions, and not every elected official agrees with every platform position. Question choices are intentionally written to present the strongest reasonable value behind competing positions rather than treating one side as the default or morally preferred answer.
